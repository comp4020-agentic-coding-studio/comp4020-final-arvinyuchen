# Crit 8 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was a question, not code. My first idea was a trip itinerary
app, and I knew it sounded flat: a shared list of places and times is what
Google Docs and a dozen planning apps already do. Asking where a group trip
actually goes wrong changed it. The hard part isn't listing places, it's a
group deciding together. Once I saw that, the brief's three rules stopped being
boxes to tick. Multi-user is the group, real-time is everyone voting at once,
and persistence is the list still being there next weekend. That turned into
Spots: one group of friends, a link and a name to join, places to explore, and
a "keen" vote that re-ranks the list live. Narrowing it to one group, not every
student or all of Canberra, is also what made the README writable. I couldn't
say what good means for "everyone", but I can for a group chat that keeps
saying "we should go somewhere".

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

This week I spent more time deciding than building. The agent could have
produced a generic places app in minutes, and it pushed back when my idea
drifted toward a city guide. The decisions that made Spots worth building were
all mine to make: who it's for, what counts as a person, what to leave out. I
want to be the developer who makes those calls deliberately and writes them
down (the README's definition of good, the rules in CLAUDE.md), rather than
accepting the median answer because it arrived quickly. Small things reinforced
that: a deploy token scoped to the wrong app, and a port already taken on my
machine, were both quick to fix only because I checked what was actually
happening before trusting it.
